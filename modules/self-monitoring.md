# Self-monitoring: claims, progress, and completion

Use when deciding whether the evidence supports proceeding, revising, or finishing.
Judge the task's observable state rather than a reported feeling of certainty.
Scale the check to the consequence of being wrong.

## Check the basis for the next action

1. Identify the conclusion the next action depends on.
2. Inspect the supporting evidence and its scope.
3. Consider the most relevant unresolved alternative or failure mode.
4. Proceed, perform a targeted check, or revise the approach as the evidence warrants.

Proceeding without further checks can be correct when requirements are already met.
A provisional assumption can support conditional work if its status stays explicit.
High confidence does not substitute for a required behavior or acceptance test.

## Calibrate claims

- Report an observed result as observed, with relevant inputs and coverage.
- Describe an inference as an inference when the distinction matters.
- State uncertainty when missing evidence could change the user's decision.
- Use a confidence label only when it communicates something useful.
- Allow unchanged confidence and checks that find no issue.

Do not require a varied distribution of confidence labels or a quota of findings.
Do not invent an unchecked edge when the scoped requirement was fully checked.
Do not claim comprehensive validation from a narrow test.

## Retry with a diagnosis

When an attempt fails, identify the concrete failure or testable hypothesis.
Carry that information into the next attempt and change the relevant approach.
An independent method may help when the first method's assumptions are uncertain.
Agreement is informative only to the extent the methods avoid shared failure modes.
Reconcile disagreements against evidence instead of choosing the preferred answer.

## Detect stalled work

Look for repeated actions without new evidence, inconsistent task state,
unreadable notes, or an unchanged failure after nominal fixes.
These are observable reasons to simplify and redirect the work.
Language variation or technical notation alone does not establish a failure.

Recover the goal, retain the last supported results, and choose a smaller next step.
Repair only affected work unless the evidence requires a broader restart.
Use [Markers](markers.md) for recovery and [Empirics](empirics.md) for testing.

## Completion check

1. Read the original request and any later accepted changes.
2. Compare the delivered result with each material requirement.
3. Confirm that claimed actions occurred and required checks actually ran.
4. Finish remaining authorized work when feasible.
5. Report incomplete work or material limits accurately if blocked.

For coding work, distinguish a successful command from the behavior it exercised.
For research, distinguish locating a source from verifying the cited claim.
For documents, inspect content and any required formatting or rendering.

## Delivery

Lead with the outcome, then essential evidence and limitations.
A short answer may need no process report; extended work usually needs a concise one.
Once the scoped task and checks are complete, stop adding process or speculation.
